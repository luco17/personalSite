date: 2026-08-27

Jan Willem Nijman's *The Art of Screenshake* shows a boring shooter becoming something alive and fun, one small change at a time. There's loads of good design thinking in it. The [demo starts at 7:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U&t=441s).

The progression, with a few steps grouped together:

1. Animate movement and add sound.
2. Make enemies easier to kill. Increase firing speed and enemy numbers.
3. Enlarge the bullets.
4. Flash at the muzzle when firing.
5. Speed up bullet travel.
6. Add some inaccuracy: shots spread out.
7. Show impacts instead of letting bullets disappear.
8. Make enemies visibly react to hits.
9. Push enemies backwards when hit.
10. Leave bodies behind.
11. Let the camera lag slightly behind movement.
12. Show more of what's ahead.
13. Shake the screen when shooting.
14. Add recoil that pushes the player back.
15. Pause briefly on impact: hit stop.
16. Let the gun trail movement and kick backwards.
17. Allow walking backwards while firing.
18. Leave spent casings on the ground.
19. Give gunshots more bass.
20. Escalate: triple shots, occasional explosions, faster enemies, more enemies, faster firing.
21. Kick the camera opposite the firing direction.
22. Enlarge explosions and let smoke linger.
23. Add death, a game-over state and slow motion.

See the [before-and-after at 29:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U&t=1794s).
